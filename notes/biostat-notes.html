Topic 01

Genome-Wide Association Studies (GWAS) Quality Control

High-throughput genetic investigations depend heavily on rigorous preprocessing pipelines to weed out false positives driven by technical artifacts. Initial filtering mandates checking missingness per individual and per marker, ensuring Hardy-Weinberg equilibrium (HWE) thresholds are maintained across control cohorts.

Fig 1.1: Pre-filtering missingness distribution metrics.
Fig 1.2: PCA adjustment visualizing population stratification.

Furthermore, population stratification remains a major confounder in human genetic association testing. By incorporating principal component (PC) covariates derived from linkage disequilibrium (LD)-pruned markers into our regression models, we safeguard statistical validity and accurately isolate true causal signals from background ancestral divergence.

Topic 02

Polygenic Risk Scores (PRS) & Polygenic Architecture

Moving past single-marker significance, polygenic risk scores aggregate thousands or millions of genetic variants to quantify an individual's inherited liability toward complex human phenotypes. Our framework implements standard clumping and thresholding (C+T) alongside advanced Bayesian shrinkage models.

Fig 2.1: Cumulative liability distribution across stratified risk deciles.

By integrating posterior effect sizes derived from tools like LDpred2, we account for local LD structures and varying causal fraction assumptions, significantly elevating predictive accuracy across diverse validation cohorts.

Topic 03

Mendelian Randomization (MR) Causal Inference

Establishing causality rather than mere correlation is imperative for translating genetic findings into therapeutic targets. Mendelian randomization leverages genetic variants as instrumental variables (IVs) to evaluate the causal effect of an exposure on a clinical outcome.

Fig 3.1: Variant effect on exposure versus outcome regression slopes.
Fig 3.2: MR-Egger intercept evaluation for horizontal pleiotropy.

To protect against violations of instrumental variable assumptions—specifically horizontal pleiotropy—our analytical pipelines systematically deploy sensitivity estimators including MR-Egger regression, weighted median models, and leave-one-out analyses.

Topic 04

Transcriptome-Wide Association (TWAS) Pipelines

Genome-wide association signals frequently localize to non-coding regions, complicating biological interpretation. Transcriptome-wide association studies bridge this gap by predicting gene expression levels from genetic panels and correlating them directly with phenotype data.

Fig 4.1: Colocalization analysis integrating eQTL weights with GWAS summary data.

Utilizing reference expression weights from panels such as GTEx alongside S-PrediXcan and FUSION scripts, we successfully prioritize putative causal genes, illuminating target mechanisms for downstream wet-lab validation loops.

Topic 05

Large-Scale Multi-Omics Data Fusion & Survival Metrics

The ultimate frontier in statistical genetics is the synthesis of disparate biological layers—genomics, transcriptomics, and proteomics—with longitudinal clinical endpoints.

Fig 5.2: Penalized regression survival stratification curves.

Our capstone pipeline utilizes regularized multivariate methods and Cox Proportional Hazards modeling to translate complex genomic scores into robust prognostic clinical markers, aligning completely with the high-caliber methodological research pursued at institutions like the University of Lausanne (UNIL FBM).
